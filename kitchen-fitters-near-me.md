## What should I check before I book a kitchen fitter?

1. **What's in the price.** Fitting units is one thing. Ask whether it covers stripping out the old kitchen, taking it away, plumbing in the sink and dishwasher, and any plastering or tiling.
2. **Who does the gas.** Only a Gas Safe registered engineer can disconnect or connect a gas hob or cooker. Ask the fitter who will do it and check the engineer on the Gas Safe Register.
3. **Who does the electrics.** In England and Wales, adding a new circuit, such as for an electric oven or hob, is notifiable under Part P of the Building Regulations. A registered electrician (such as NICEIC or NAPIT) can self-certify it and give you a certificate.
4. **Waste.** If they take the old kitchen away, they should hold a waste carrier licence.
5. **Recent reviews and photos.** Look for kitchens like yours, not just star ratings.
6. **Who supplies the units.** Some fitters supply the kitchen, many fit one you have bought. Be clear before you agree a price.

## Stay safe while the old kitchen comes out

- If you smell gas at any point, open windows, don't use switches or flames, and call the free National Gas Emergency number on 0800 111 999.
- Don't leave a gas pipe capped by anyone who isn't Gas Safe registered.
- Keep water away from sockets and loose wiring while the sink is disconnected.

## FAQ

**Can a kitchen fitter connect my gas hob?**
Only if they are Gas Safe registered themselves. If not, they should bring in someone who is.

**Do I need building regulations sign-off for a new kitchen?**
Often not for like-for-like units, but new electrical circuits and some plumbing or structural changes can need it. Ask your fitter and electrician what paperwork you'll get.

**Should I get more than one price?**
Yes. Two or three written prices for the same scope make it easy to compare.
