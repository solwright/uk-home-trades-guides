Fuse box sparking at night is one of the most frightening things a UK homeowner can hear or see. It often happens in the evening, when the oven, kettle, shower and heaters are all pulling power at once and a weak connection in the consumer unit finally gives way. This page is a plan for tonight and for the repair visit tomorrow. It is not a guide to working on the board.

*Written by Sol Wright, Tradehand. General safety information for UK homes. Not DIY electrical instruction.*

## Tonight: the first five minutes

1. **Fire, smoke, melting plastic or a strong burning smell?** Get everyone out, close the door behind you and call **999**. Do not go back for the board.
2. **Sparks or crackling but no fire?** If your hands and the floor are dry and the main switch is well away from the sparking, switch the main switch off once.
3. **Main switch is the part sparking, or you would have to reach past it?** Do not touch the board. Call an emergency electrician and tell them you cannot isolate it.
4. **Water on or above the board?** Do not touch it. Stop the leak at the stopcock if that is safe, and call an emergency electrician from another room.
5. **Damage at the meter, the sealed cut-out or the incoming cable?** That belongs to your network operator. Call **105**, the free UK power network number.

Once it is off, leave it off. A board that went quiet after one spark can still have a burnt connection inside.

## Why it so often happens in the evening

Arcing in a consumer unit is usually heat at a loose or worn connection. Heat rises with current, and current peaks on cold evenings when cooking, showers, tumble dryers and plug-in heaters overlap. A terminal that coped all summer can start to crackle in the first cold week of autumn. That is also why the noise can come and go: it follows the load, not the clock.

Common causes an electrician will look for:

- Loose terminal screws on breakers, RCDs or the main switch
- A worn or failing breaker or RCD
- A scorched busbar (the metal strip that feeds the breakers)
- Water getting in from a leak, condensation or a board under a bathroom
- Cables not fully seated after earlier work

## Staying safe and warm with the power off

A night without power is uncomfortable, but it is far safer than a board that might arc again.

- Use torches or battery lanterns, not candles near curtains or children.
- Keep the fridge and freezer closed. Most will hold their temperature for several hours.
- Do not run an extension lead from a neighbour, a caravan hook-up or a generator into the house. That can back-feed the wiring and put the electrician at risk.
- If anyone relies on powered medical equipment, tell the electrician and call 105 to ask about the Priority Services Register.
- If the gas boiler needs power to run, it will stop. Extra layers, hot water bottles filled before you switched off, and one warm room help until the morning.

## Tomorrow: what the electrician checks

A competent electrician should work through the board in a clear order:

1. **Safe isolation.** They prove the board is dead before touching it. If it cannot be isolated, they arrange for the network operator to pull the main fuse.
2. **Find the source.** Burn marks, melted insulation, a discoloured breaker or a scorched busbar usually show where the arcing started.
3. **Repair or replace.** One damaged breaker on an otherwise sound, modern board can sometimes be replaced. A plastic board, heavy scorching, or a unit with no RCD protection usually points to a full replacement.
4. **Test before switching on.** Every circuit should be tested before power goes back on, so a hidden fault elsewhere is not left behind.

A full board change often takes around four to eight hours, depending on the number of circuits. A sparking board does not automatically mean a rewire. Testing shows whether the fixed wiring is still sound.

## Plastic or metal board?

Since January 2016, BS 7671 (the IET Wiring Regulations) has required new consumer units in homes to have a non-combustible enclosure, which in practice usually means metal. If your board is plastic and it has sparked, ask whether replacing the unit makes more sense than swapping a single part.

## Questions to ask before they leave

- What caused it, and can you show me a photo of the damage?
- Was anything else on the board loose or overheating?
- Did every circuit pass testing?
- Which certificate am I getting for this work?

**Certificates matter.** In England and Wales, replacing a consumer unit is notifiable building work. An electrician registered with a competent person scheme can self-certify it and should give you an Electrical Installation Certificate, with a building regulations compliance certificate following from the scheme. Smaller repairs usually come with a Minor Electrical Installation Works Certificate. Keep them with your house documents; you will want them if you sell or let the property.

## Tenants and landlords

- **Tenants:** switch off if it is safe, then tell your landlord or agent straight away by phone and in writing.
- **Landlords in England:** you must keep the electrical installation safe and have it inspected and tested at least every five years (an EICR). A sparking board is an urgent repair, not something to leave until the next inspection.

## Things not to do

- Do not open the cover "just to look", even with the main switch off. The supply side can still be live.
- Do not throw water on an electrical fire.
- Do not keep resetting a breaker or RCD that keeps tripping. It is doing its job.
- Do not tape or wedge breakers on.
- Do not touch the sealed cut-out, main fuse or meter tails.
