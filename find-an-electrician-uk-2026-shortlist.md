## What finding an electrician actually is (and what it is not)

<div markdown="0"><p>Finding an electrician means three separate jobs: finding someone local and available, getting a fair price, and confirming they are competent for the work. Most services are good at one or two of these, not all three.</p>
<p>It is not the same as checking qualifications. A listing, a badge or a good review does not tell you an electrician is registered to self-certify notifiable work under Building Regulations in England and Wales. For that, use the Registered Competent Person Electrical register or ask for NICEIC or NAPIT membership and check it.</p>
<p>It is also not an emergency service. If there is burning, sparking or a shock risk, turn off at the consumer unit if it is safe and call an emergency electrician directly. Online quote forms are for planned and routine work: EICRs, extra sockets, lighting, fault finding and consumer unit changes.</p>
<p>In Basingstoke and across Hampshire the same rules apply as anywhere in England. Pick a way to find and price the job, then confirm the electrician before they start.</p></div>

Registered Competent Person Electrical register, best for checking an electrician is registered for work in your home</h3><p>The Registered Competent Person Electrical register is the official search for electricians registered with a Government-approved Competent Person Scheme in England and Wales. You enter a postcode to find registered electricians near you, or search a company name to check whether someone you are already considering is listed.</p>
<p>Every electrician on it is registered with a scheme operator and authorised to self-certify that their work complies with Building Regulations. The register says their work is regularly assessed. It also lists inspectors registered to carry out electrical safety reports in homes. The single mark and register went live on 30 June 2014.</p>
<p>Where it is weaker: it is a register, not a booking service. There is no price before a visit, no reviews on the register itself, and it covers England and Wales only.</p>
<p><strong>Best suited for:</strong> anyone having notifiable work done, such as a consumer unit change or new circuit. Use it alongside whatever service you find the electrician through.</p><h3>3. Checkatrade, best for a directory with published background checks</h3><p>Checkatrade is a long-running UK trade directory, founded in 1998 according to its own site data. Homeowners search by trade and postcode, read reviews, and contact or book a trade from their profile.</p>
<p>Its published numbers: every trade passes up to 12 checks before joining, including photo ID, proof of address, qualifications such as Gas Safe or NICEIC, personal and business CCJ checks, director checks and financial checks. Checks are repeated in the background. The homepage states 6M+ real reviews, a £1,000 guarantee on eligible jobs (terms apply), and an app rating of 4.8 from 114K+ ratings. Rated People is now part of the Checkatrade Group.</p>
<p>Where it is weaker: trades pay a membership to appear, so the directory is a paid listing service from the trade's side. There is no instant price for your job before a visit.</p>
<p><strong>Best suited for:</strong> homeowners who want a recognised brand, published checks and a guarantee. It suits larger jobs where checks matter more than speed.</p><h3>4. MyBuilder, best for posting a job and choosing from trades who are interested</h3><p>MyBuilder works the other way round from a directory. You post the job for free, MyBuilder alerts relevant tradespeople, and those who are interested express interest. You then start chats with the ones you like.</p>
<p>Its published process: contact details are shared only when you say so, interested trades send you a quote or estimated cost, and you can check profiles, work history and reviews before choosing. Customers review completed jobs. On the trade side, MyBuilder charges a fee per job a trade responds to, shown before they respond.</p>
<p>Where it is weaker: you wait for interest rather than getting a price straight away, and the quotes you see depend on which trades respond.</p>
<p><strong>Best suited for:</strong> homeowners with a defined project who are happy to post once and compare a few responses. It works well for building and renovation work.</p><h3>5. Rated People, best for comparing up to 3 quotes from rated trades</h3><p>Rated People is a job-posting site now part of the Checkatrade Group. You fill in a request with a description, photos or videos, and tradespeople send quotes.</p>
<p>Its published numbers: free to post with no hidden fees, every tradesperson is rated and reviewed, and you get up to 3 quotes to compare.</p>
<p>Where it is weaker: the trades who quote are the ones who choose to respond, and you will not see a price until they do.</p>
<p><strong>Best suited for:</strong> homeowners who want a small number of quotes to compare without calling round. Add photos to get closer estimates.</p><h3>6. Bark, best for services beyond the core trades</h3><p>Bark is a national marketplace covering a wide range of services, not just trades. You answer a few questions about the job and Bark searches its professionals for matches.</p>
<p>Its published process: Bark says it searches thousands of professionals, and professionals are only given your details once they have sent you a quote. On the trade side, Bark sells credits that professionals spend to respond to leads.</p>
<p>Where it is weaker: it is broad rather than trade-specialist, and it does not publish trade-specific checks on the pages we read.</p>
<p><strong>Best suited for:</strong> homeowners who need something outside the usual trades, or who want to compare several types of provider in one place.</p></div>
