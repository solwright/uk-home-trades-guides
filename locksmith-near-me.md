Searching "locksmith near me" usually means one of three things: you're locked out, a lock has broken or jammed, or you want the locks changed after moving in or losing keys.

Short answer: if anyone is at risk, call 999 first. Otherwise, ring two or three local locksmiths, get the full price (call-out, labour and parts) before anyone starts, and ask them to try to open the door without drilling the lock. Locksmiths aren't licensed in the UK, so ask for ID, a business address and recent reviews before you let anyone work.

## First, is anyone at risk?

- **A child, a vulnerable person or a pet locked inside, or something cooking on the hob.** Call 999. The emergency services can get in faster than a locksmith.
- **Signs of a break-in.** Don't go in. Step away and call 999 if someone may still be inside, or 101 if they've gone.
- **Locked out in the cold or dark.** Wait somewhere safe and lit while you ring round.

## How do I check a locksmith before they start?

1. **Get the total price first.** Call-out, labour, any new lock and VAT if they charge it. Ask what happens to the price if the job takes longer.
2. **Ask how they'll get in.** A good locksmith tries non-destructive entry (picking or bypassing) first. Drilling should be the last resort, and they should tell you before they do it.
3. **Ask for ID and a business address.** Anyone can call themselves a locksmith in the UK, so this matters.
4. **Check reviews and how long they've traded.** Membership of a trade body such as the Master Locksmiths Association is voluntary, but it's a useful sign.
5. **Be wary of very low phone quotes.** A cheap headline price that grows on the doorstep is the most common complaint.
6. **Get a receipt** that shows what was done and what was fitted.

## Changing the locks after moving in or losing keys

If you've just moved in or lost keys with your address on, changing the cylinder is usually quick. If your home insurance names a standard such as BS3621 for door locks, ask the locksmith to fit a lock that meets it. Renting? Check your tenancy agreement and tell your landlord before changing locks.

## FAQ

**Will a locksmith need to drill my lock?**
Not usually. Most doors can be opened without damage. Ask before they start.

**Do I need to prove I live there?**
A careful locksmith will ask. Have ID or a bill handy once you're inside.

**Should I get more than one price?**
Yes, even in a hurry. Two quick calls can save a lot.
