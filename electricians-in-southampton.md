Looking for electricians in Southampton? Sort out anything unsafe first, then compare a few local electricians on the same job.

Short answer: if you smell burning, see sparks or scorch marks, or a socket or switch feels hot, stop using it and switch off at the fuse box only if you can do it safely with dry hands. If there's smoke or fire, get out and call 999. For a power cut across your street, call 105 first. Then compare two or three Southampton electricians who are registered with a scheme such as NICEIC or NAPIT and get the price in writing before work starts.

## How do I pick a good electrician?

- **Check the register.** NICEIC and NAPIT members can self-certify notifiable work under Part P, so you get the right paperwork.
- **Match the job.** If you need an EV charger, pick someone who fits them. Same for EICRs or emergency lighting.
- **Read recent reviews** for detail about how the job went, not just the stars.
- **Get the price in writing** and ask how they'll handle anything extra they find.
- **Ask for the certificate** at the end, such as an Electrical Installation Certificate or a Minor Works certificate.

## What should I not do myself?

Don't open up the fuse box, a socket or a light fitting, and don't reset a breaker that keeps tripping again and again. Something is wrong if it keeps going. Leave it off and tell the electrician exactly what happened.

**Do Southampton electricians charge a call-out fee?**
Some do, especially for emergencies. Ask before they come out, and ask for the full price up front.

**Do landlords in Southampton need an EICR?**
Yes. Landlords in England need an EICR at least every five years, and sooner if the last report says so.
