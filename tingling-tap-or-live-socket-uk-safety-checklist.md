Tingling tap or live socket at home? Here is a UK safety checklist for the first hour, written for people who have just felt something they should not have and want to know what to do next.

A tingle from a kitchen tap, a buzz off a radiator, or a jolt from a plug socket faceplate all point to the same thing: metal that should be safe to touch is carrying mains current. That is not something to sleep on or "keep an eye on". The good news is that the first steps are simple and none of them involve taking anything apart.

*Written by Sol Wright, Tradehand. General safety information for UK homes. Not DIY electrical instruction.*

## The first five minutes

**People before fittings.** If anyone has been knocked down, burned, held by the current, or is confused or struggling to breathe, ring **999**. If they might still be touching the tap, socket or appliance, do not grab them. Cut the power at the consumer unit first, but only if you can reach it safely and it is dry.

Even a short shock that "felt like nothing" is worth a call to **NHS 111** for advice. Chest pain, a racing or irregular heartbeat, or a burn mark where the current went in or out are reasons to get help straight away.

Once everyone is safe, turn your attention to the house.

## Make it safe without opening anything

Think of this as switching off and stepping back, not fixing.

1. **Stop using whatever gave the shock.** Leave the socket empty. Do not run that tap, shower or bath.
2. **Keep others away.** Children and pets especially, from the socket, the sink, radiators and any bare pipework.
3. **Switch off at the consumer unit if it is safe to do so.** For one socket, turn off that circuit's breaker. For a tap, radiator or pipework shock, use the main switch, because the fault could be on any circuit.
4. **Use labelled isolators if you have them.** An immersion heater or electric shower often has its own switch. Turn it off if you can reach it without touching anything wet or metal.
5. **Leave covers on.** No unscrewing faceplates, no tester screwdrivers, no taking the back off the washing machine.

Switching off is a holding step. If the tingle stops, that does not mean the fault is fixed. It just means the circuit feeding it is off.

## Why a socket goes live

A plug socket should only be live on the inside. When the faceplate, the switch, or the plug pins you touch while plugging in give you a shock, an electrician will usually be looking for one of these:

- An earth wire that has come loose or was never connected, which matters most with metal faceplates
- Cable insulation damaged behind the socket, for example by a screw, a nail or heat
- A cracked or broken front exposing live parts
- Wiring mistakes, such as reversed polarity
- Worn contacts that arc as a plug goes in or comes out
- A wider earthing fault if more than one socket on the circuit is affected

A quick way to tell it is not static: static is one sharp snap after walking across carpet. A socket that tingles every time you touch it, shocks you with nothing plugged in, or feels warm, smells of burning or looks scorched is a genuine fault.

## Why a tap or radiator goes live

Taps, radiators and pipes are metal, and metal carries current. The tap is rarely the cause. It is where you notice the fault. Common reasons include:

- A failing immersion heater element or electric shower leaking current into the water system
- Missing or loose **main protective bonding**, the green and yellow earth clamps on the incoming water and gas pipes
- A water-connected appliance, such as a washing machine or dishwasher, with an internal fault
- A damaged cable resting against a pipe under the floor or behind a kitchen unit
- In flats and terraces, a fault next door travelling through shared pipework

If taps, radiators and other metalwork all feel live even with your main switch off, the problem may be on the supply side, such as a broken neutral in the network cable feeding the property. That needs the **Distribution Network Operator (DNO)**, the company that runs your local power network. Ring **105**, the free national power cut and emergency number, as well as getting an electrician.

## Who to call, and how quickly

| Situation | Who to call | Urgency |
|---|---|---|
| Someone hurt, unconscious or badly burned | 999 | Immediately |
| Tingle from one socket or faceplate | Electrician | Same day if you can |
| Metal faceplate live with nothing plugged in | Electrician | Urgent |
| Several sockets giving shocks | Electrician | Urgent |
| Socket hot, scorched or smells of burning | Electrician | Urgent, there is a fire risk too |
| One tap tingles, often when the immersion or shower is on | Electrician | Urgent |
| All metalwork live, even with the main switch off | Electrician, plus 105 for the DNO | Urgent |
| Consumer unit wet or hard to reach safely | Electrician | Do not touch anything until they arrive |

## Things people tell themselves (and why they are wrong)

- **"It was just static."** Static does not repeat every time you turn on a tap.
- **"The RCD did not trip, so it is fine."** Some earth faults and unusual current paths can shock you without tripping an RCD.
- **"It is a plastic socket, it cannot shock me."** Plastic does not conduct like metal, but damaged contacts and wiring inside still can.
- **"I switched the immersion off and it stopped."** That removes one symptom. It does not make the installation safe.
- **"I will just have a quick cold shower."** Wet skin, bare feet and live pipework is exactly the combination to avoid.

## Finding the right electrician

Look for a **registered electrician**. Many are registered with a government-approved competent person scheme such as NICEIC or NAPIT, and you can check a name on the scheme's own search or on the government's Registered Competent Person Electrical register.

When you call, give them the facts: which socket or tap, whether anything was plugged in or running, whether the RCD tripped, and whether anything else felt live. Ask them to tell you plainly whether it was one faulty fitting, a fault on the circuit, or a supply problem for the DNO. For new circuits or consumer unit work in England and Wales, Part P of the Building Regulations applies, so ask for the certificate.

If you do not have someone you trust, [Tradehand](https://tradehand.com/?utm_source=github_pages&utm_medium=parasite&utm_campaign=shock-guide) is free for homeowners to post a job and hear from local electricians.

## If you rent, or you are the landlord

- **Tenants:** tell your landlord or agent in writing straight away, with the time, the fitting and what you were doing. Stop using it.
- **Landlords and agents:** treat it as an urgent electrical safety issue that day. Get a competent electrician out and keep a record of the report and the repair.
- **Flats:** ask neighbours if they have felt anything similar, and let the managing agent know. Shared risers and pipework can carry a fault between homes.

## What the visit usually involves

An electrician will normally isolate the circuit and prove it dead, then check the socket or fittings and cables involved. Expect tests for earth continuity, polarity, insulation resistance and RCD operation, plus a look at main bonding and any supplementary bonding in the bathroom. If a tap was involved, they will often test the immersion, shower and water-connected appliances for leakage. They should repair the fault, restore earthing and bonding, retest, and tell you if the DNO needs to look at the supply.

Prices depend on the property, where you live and how urgent the call-out is, so ask for a quote for your specific job.

## One-minute checklist

- Anyone hurt? Ring 999. Do not touch someone still in contact.
- Stop using the socket, tap or shower.
- Switch off at the consumer unit only if it is dry and safe to reach.
- Leave faceplates and appliances closed.
- Book an electrician, urgently if metal is live, several sockets are affected, or there are burning signs.
- All metalwork live with the main switch off? Ring 105 as well.
- Do not use it again until it has been tested and repaired.
