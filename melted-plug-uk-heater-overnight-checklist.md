Melted plug after a heater overnight is one of the most common Instant Quote briefs UK electricians see in the first cold snap. An oil-filled heater, fan heater or tumble dryer left on for hours through a wall socket or an extension board overheats a weak connection. The plastic softens. The fuse often never blows. This page is a checklist for the hours before the electrician arrives. It is not DIY electrical instruction.

*Written by Sol Wright, Tradehand. General safety information for UK homes. Not DIY electrical instruction.*

## Stop first

1. **Smoke or flame?** Get everyone out, close the door and call **999**.
2. **No smoke, but the plug or faceplate is melted, brown or warped?** Switch the circuit off at the consumer unit. Use the main switch if you are not sure which breaker it is.
3. **Do not yank a hot or fused plug.** Leave it to cool with the power off.
4. Once cool and still off, remove the plug. Put that socket and that appliance out of use.
5. Check other plugs on the same lead or circuit for browning or warping.

Leave the circuit off until the socket has been replaced and tested. Plastic that has changed shape has already been past a safe temperature.

## Why heaters and overnight loads do this

Heat builds where metal meets metal: a loose terminal in a rewireable plug, a fuse sitting badly in its clips, worn socket contacts that no longer grip the pins, or damaged strands at the cord grip. That heat can happen at normal current, so the BS 1362 fuse and the circuit breaker often stay quiet. "The fuse never blew" does not mean the outlet was safe.

High-load appliances that run for hours are the usual trigger:

- Fan and oil-filled heaters
- Tumble dryers
- Washing machines
- Kettles and irons used repeatedly on a tired socket or adaptor

Extension leads and multi-way adaptors add more connections that can overheat. Electrical Safety First advises against overloading sockets and leads. If the board itself felt hot, treat the wall outlet as suspect too.

## What not to do while you wait

- Do not fit a new plug and keep using the same wall socket.
- Do not move the heater to the other half of a double socket on the same faceplate.
- Do not run an extension from another room as a permanent workaround for the damaged outlet.
- Do not open the socket faceplate to "have a look" unless you are competent to isolate and prove dead.
- Do not switch the circuit back on for a quick test if the faceplate is warped or scorched.

A new BS 1363 body in cooked contacts is how the second melt happens.

## What to tell the electrician on the phone

1. "Melted plug in a wall socket" (say if the faceplate is also browned or warped).
2. Which appliance was plugged in, and roughly how long it had been on.
3. Whether you isolated the circuit at the consumer unit.
4. Whether an extension lead or adaptor was involved.
5. Whether anyone smelled burning after you switched off.

Send a clear photo of the plug and faceplate if you can. That helps them bring the right socket and plan for cable damage behind the back box.

## What a good repair usually includes

A competent electrician should:

1. Isolate and remove the melted plug and socket safely.
2. Inspect the back box, cable ends and insulation for heat damage, cutting back to sound cable if needed.
3. Fit a new socket and tighten terminations correctly.
4. Test the circuit before leaving it in use.
5. Check the appliance lead and plug, and fit a correctly fused BS 1363 plug if the appliance is otherwise sound.
6. Advise on keeping high-load heaters off tired leads and adaptors.

Ask them to show the new faceplate seated cleanly and to confirm the appliance lead is sound. "New plug only" jobs come back the next cold evening when cooked contacts were left in.

## Landlord and tenant

**Tenants:** tell the landlord or managing agent once it is safe. Share the photo, say the circuit is off, and do not keep using the outlet while you wait.

**Landlords:** treat it as urgent. Ask for socket replacement plus inspection of the cable and back box, not a plug swap. Keep any Minor Works or test paperwork.
