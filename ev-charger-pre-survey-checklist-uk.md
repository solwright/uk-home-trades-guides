Before an EV charger installer visits your UK home, you can save time and get a better quote by knowing your parking spot, your rough cable route, what your consumer unit looks like and which charger you want. The installer then checks your supply and earthing and prices the job on survey. A typical home charge point costs around £800 to £1,200 installed, according to Energy Saving Trust.

*Written by Sol Wright, Tradehand. Updated 10 November 2026. General information for UK homes. Not DIY electrical instruction.*

## What should I do before the survey?

Things you can safely do yourself:

- **Pick where the car parks.** Driveway, side wall, garage. Note how far that is from your consumer unit.
- **Take photos.** Your consumer unit with the cover closed, the meter, the outside wall where you want the charger and the route between them. Photos only. Do not open anything.
- **Note your main fuse rating** if it is printed on the visible label of the service head. Do not remove covers or seals to find it.
- **Choose a charger shortlist.** Tethered or untethered, and the power you need. A 7kW unit suits most single-phase homes.
- **Check grant rules.** Look at the current GOV.UK electric vehicle chargepoint grant pages before you order.

Things you should never do: open the consumer unit, cut the meter seal, run cable or drill near the supply. That is all electrician work.

## What will the electrician check?

1. **Supply capacity.** Whether your supply and consumer unit can take the charger, or need work first.
2. **Earthing arrangement.** Many UK homes have a PME supply. The installer must choose a compliant protective arrangement for the charger and site. Not every PME install needs an earth rod.
3. **Cable route and distance.** Longer routes, trenches and drives add time and cost.
4. **Protective measures.** The charge point should sit on its own protected circuit back to the consumer unit.

## What affects the final price?

Per Tradehand's cost guide, the main drivers are the charger model and power, distance and cable route from the consumer unit, electrical upgrades and protective measures, and groundworks, access and regional labour rates. Quotes include the charger, standard labour and cabling for the quoted scope, plus testing, setup and certification.

## Charging before the charge point goes in

If you charge from a 13A socket for now, use the car maker's cable straight into a socket in good condition, never through an extension lead or reel. If the plug or socket gets hot, smells of burning or shows marks, stop, switch off at the socket if safe, and get it checked before using it again. Smoke or fire: get out and call 999.

## What to ask on the day

- Is my supply big enough, or do I need an upgrade first?
- Which earthing arrangement will you use, and why?
- Exactly where will the cable run?
- Is any groundwork included?
- Will I get an electrical installation certificate, and will you handle the notifications?
- Are you registered with a competent person scheme, and are you an OZEV authorised installer if I want a grant?

## Quick FAQ

**Can I be at work during the install?**
Someone usually needs to be in for the start and the handover. Ask your installer.

**Will the power go off?**
Usually for part of the job while the circuit is connected. Your installer will tell you how long.

**I rent. Can I get a charger?**
Get your landlord's written permission first.
