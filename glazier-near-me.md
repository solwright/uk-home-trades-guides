## First, make the broken glass safe

- **Keep children and pets out of the room** until the glass is cleared.
- **Wear thick gloves and proper shoes.** Don't pick up shards with bare hands, and use a dustpan and brush, not a vacuum, for the big pieces.
- **Don't pull loose shards out of the frame.** Leave them for the glazier. If a cracked pane is still in one piece, criss-cross it with strong tape so it can't fall.
- **Cover the gap from inside** with a board or thick plastic sheet if it's raining or you can't secure the window. Emergency boarding up is one of the jobs glaziers do most.
- **Wrap broken glass in newspaper or cardboard** and label it before it goes in the bin, so nobody gets cut.
- **Looks like a break-in?** Don't touch anything. Call 999 if someone may still be there, or 101 if they've gone, and keep the crime reference for your insurer.

## Does it need to be safety glass?

In the UK, glass in doors, in panels next to doors, and in low panes close to the floor is in a "critical location". Replacement glass there should be safety glass, usually toughened or laminated, so it breaks safely if someone falls into it. Tell the glazier where the pane is and ask them to confirm what they'll fit. Toughened glass normally has a small mark etched into one corner.

## Just the glass, or the whole window?

- **Misted double glazing** usually means the seal on the glass unit has failed. Often only the sealed unit needs replacing, not the frame.
- **Replacing the whole window or door, frame and all,** has to meet Building Regulations in England and Wales. Most homeowners use an installer registered with a competent person scheme such as FENSA or CERTASS, who signs it off and sends you a certificate. Keep it for when you sell.
- **Replacing only the glass** doesn't need that sign off, but the safety glass rule above still applies.

## What to ask a glazier before they start

1. **What's the total price,** including any boarding up, the new glass and taking the old glass away?
2. **What glass will you fit?** Toughened, laminated, or a new sealed unit, and does it match the rest?
3. **Is there a guarantee** on the glass or sealed unit, and for how long?
4. **When can you come,** and can you board up today if the glass can't be fitted straight away?
5. **Can I see ID and a business address,** and where can I read your reviews?

## FAQ

**Can I tape a cracked window and leave it?**
Only for a short time. Tape stops it falling, but a cracked pane can still give way, so book the repair.

**Do I need a glazier or a window fitter?**
A glazier for broken, cracked or misted glass. A window fitter for new frames, though many do both.

**Should I get more than one price?**
Yes. A photo and the size of the pane make quotes quick to compare.
