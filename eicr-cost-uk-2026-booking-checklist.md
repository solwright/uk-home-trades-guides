Booking an Electrical Installation Condition Report is easy to get wrong in the enquiry email. One electrician quotes inspection only. Another bundles "we'll sort anything we find". A third adds VAT without saying so. You then compare three numbers that are not the same job.

This checklist is for UK landlords and homeowners who want comparable quotes before they book. Cost figures below match Tradehand's live hub only.

*Written by Sol Wright, Tradehand. General information for UK homes. Not legal advice or DIY electrical instruction.*

## The cost band you are aiming at

Tradehand's EICR cost page (last updated October 2026) cites Which?: for a **standard three-bedroom semi-detached house**, an electrical inspection report typically costs **£100 to £330**, including labour and materials and **excluding VAT**. That figure comes from a Which? survey of **19 Trusted Traders** in **November 2025**. Every job is still priced on survey, and the hub notes quotes are **likely to be at the upper end in London or the South East**.

Use that band as a reference for a clean, inspection-only job on a similar property. It is not a guaranteed price for your house.

## What should be inside an inspection quote

Ask the electrician to confirm, in writing, that the fee covers:

1. Inspection and testing of the **agreed fixed installation** (spell out dwellings, outbuildings, landlord supply, common parts).
2. A **written report** with observations and an overall outcome.
3. Whether the figure is **excluding or including VAT**.

Usually **not** inside that fee: identified remedial work. The hub FAQ is blunt: repairs are usually not included; ask for identified remedial work to be itemised separately. Rewires, consumer unit changes and new circuits are separate jobs.

## Eight questions that keep quotes comparable

Copy these into your enquiry. Same questions to every electrician means you can actually rank the replies.

1. **What property and scope are you quoting?** Full dwelling, flat only, outbuildings, EV circuit, landlord supply?
2. **Is this inspection and report only?** Please exclude remedial labour and materials from this figure.
3. **Is the price excluding VAT?** If you are VAT-registered, show VAT separately.
4. **What would push this above the typical three-bed semi band?** Extra circuits, poor access, multiple boards, larger floor area?
5. **How long do you expect to be on site?** Time depends on size, circuit count, access and testing extent; you want their estimate for *your* brief.
6. **What do you need from me before the visit?** Previous EICR, consumer unit photos, keys, meter cupboard access, parking notes.
7. **If the report needs remediations, will you itemise them separately?** Ask for a written schedule after the inspection, not a vague "we'll sort it".
8. **How do you evidence competence for inspection work?** Scheme membership and inspection experience matter; cheap alone is not enough.

If two replies look far apart, the gap is often scope, VAT or bundled repairs, not a mystery bargain.

## Landlord checklist

- Confirm which legal regime applies to your property and tenure. Inspection intervals depend on tenure, jurisdiction, the previous report and installation condition. Do not treat a cost guide as your statutory calendar.
- Attach the last EICR if you have one. The hub's "how to pay less" tips start with giving the electrician the previous report and circuit details.
- State the outcome you need (for example a report suitable for a letting agent file) without asking anyone to invent a satisfactory result.
- Keep the inspection quote and the remedial quote as two documents. That protects you when a managing agent or tenant asks what was paid for the report itself.

## Homeowner and buyer checklist

- Say why you need the report (sale, mortgage, peace of mind after a renovation). Conveyancers sometimes care about wording on the outcome.
- Photograph the consumer unit and any outbuildings so the electrician can see board type and rough circuit count before visiting.
- Do not assume a low inspection fee includes bringing everything up to the latest Wiring Regulations. Remedials are usually extra.
- Compare like with like: same scope, same VAT treatment, remediations excluded from the headline figure.

## Red flags when reading replies

- A single price that says "EICR and any repairs" with no breakdown.
- No mention of VAT on either side of the number.
- Scope that skips outbuildings you thought were included, or quietly adds common parts you did not ask for.
- Pressure to accept remediations before you have seen the written observations.

None of that means the electrician is wrong for your job. It means the quote is not yet comparable to the Which? three-bed semi band.
